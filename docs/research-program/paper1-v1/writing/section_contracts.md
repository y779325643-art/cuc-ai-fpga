# Section contracts

## Section: Problem and motivation

- Purpose: explain why project-level correctness and verifier separation remain
  open after repository-level agent convergence.
- Inputs: HORIZON reader, current code/evidence, failure reports.
- Allowed claims: repository loops are strong; visible convergence can overfit;
  FPGA projects add cross-file and physical obligations.
- Forbidden claims: “agents cannot repair RTL”; “SR2FPGA is the first tool loop.”
- Required evidence: closest-work comparison and at least one concrete current
  failure/evidence boundary.
- Validation checklist: distinguish the product problem of incomplete inputs
  from the Paper-1 ready-requirement estimand; central gaps are honest
  disposition, typed project contracts, and hidden correctness, not generic
  automation.

## Section: Related work and differentiation

- Purpose: locate SR2FPGA by task unit, representation, oracle privilege,
  learning locus, physical depth, and reproducibility.
- Inputs: systematic literature map, HORIZON, direct/repair RTL systems,
  IR/structured generation, tool-feedback learning.
- Allowed claims: the combination is a candidate differentiation.
- Forbidden claims: “first hardware IR,” “first repository agent,” or absence of
  prior art without a formal search.
- Required evidence: matched-work table and precise capability boundaries.
- Validation checklist: HORIZON motivates M1; M1 is not called HORIZON-like until
  its project-pack, evaluator, checkpoint/history, session, and trace semantics
  are implemented.

## Section: Method

- Purpose: define shared requirement disposition, System-HIS, the method-neutral
  project contract, deterministic contract skeleton, episode pack, trajectory
  fields, and information boundaries.
- Inputs: canonical contracts and implemented runtime.
- Allowed claims: what code implements and what the frozen M0-M5 conditions test.
- Forbidden claims: full schema/W1-W10 enforcement, Tier-B decoding, model
  training, or learned-model performance.
- Required evidence: contract versions, algorithm/pseudocode, edit surfaces, and
  provenance rules.
- Validation checklist: user facts, defaults, assumptions, unresolved facts,
  deterministic/model-generated/repair-visible/trusted/hidden regions are
  distinguishable; Direct is not required to emit System-HIS.

## Section: Experimental protocol

- Purpose: freeze questions, treatments, splits, budgets, metrics, statistics,
  and stopping rules before final runs.
- Inputs: canonical Paper-1 spec, ambiguity-audit commitments, and versioned
  manifests.
- Allowed claims: protocol design only until results exist.
- Forbidden claims: cherry-picked families, post-outcome metric changes, or
  canonical-HIS access in headline end-to-end conditions.
- Required evidence: exact manifests, hashes, model/tool versions, decoding
  seeds, paired-block identities/shared checkpoints, sample counts, exclusions,
  artifact-derived G3 acceptance, and a verifier-derived G4b authorization
  receipt. Target-profile pairs, invariants, change rules, and compliance
  commitments are required only when the optional extension is enabled.
- Validation checklist: A_req is disjoint from VPP; M0-M5 privileges and global
  budgets are explicit; confirmatory gatekeeping applies once to the equal-family
  macro estimator; paired methods share the frozen initial artifacts named by
  v1.6; each hidden evaluator commits at most one semantic outcome on an
  immutable terminal candidate and is inaccessible during generation/repair.

## Section: Results

- Purpose: report ambiguity disposition/false proceed separately from hidden
  verified project success, failure transitions, convergence and episode cost,
  robustness, and representative L4 depth. Complete-pair `TPA-L4` appears only
  when the optional extension was enabled before outcomes.
- Inputs: immutable run artifacts and analysis scripts.
- Allowed claims: predeclared comparisons with intervals and per-family results.
- Forbidden claims: counting `not_run` as failure, infrastructure failure as
  design failure, visible pass as hidden correctness, or deterministic references
  as model-generated output.
- Required evidence: audit/VPP run IDs, ambiguity confusion matrix and bounds,
  aggregate and per-family project tables, uncertainty, attrition/accounting
  diagram, all-axis episode cost, hidden completion/missingness bounds, and
  negative cases. An enabled extension additionally requires its full pair
  denominator, compliance outcomes, and pair-cost availability.
- Validation checklist: totals reconcile from episode to paper table.

## Section: Discussion and threats

- Purpose: explain what caused gains/failures and bound generalization.
- Inputs: ablations, cost curves, leakage audit, representative L4 subset,
  optional profile-pair evidence when enabled, and negative examples.
- Allowed claims: mechanism-consistent interpretations labeled as such.
- Forbidden claims: broad FPGA deployment, arbitrary requirement understanding,
  or general physical realizability.
- Required evidence: alternative explanations and result-contingent paper track.
- Validation checklist: every limitation changes either a claim or a future test.

## Section: Reproducibility and artifacts

- Purpose: make benchmark, episode artifacts, configs, and evidence auditable.
- Inputs: manifests, licenses, checksums, environment capture, released scripts.
- Allowed claims: exactly what is public, internal-only, or commercially restricted.
- Forbidden claims: public reproducibility while required data remain
  `internal_only` or `review_required`.
- Required evidence: release IDs, license decision, hashes, environment/tool
  versions, and a clean-checkout reproduction command.
- Validation checklist: no credential, private path, hidden oracle, or vendor
  artifact leaks into a public release.
